Question & approach

The question

Can a trained probabilistic circuit become more robust to distribution shift without its original training data or retraining from scratch?

The approach

PeTeR addresses this question through post-training robustification. As a co-author, I contributed experiment execution, result validation, engineering support, and supplementary experiment materials. In subsequent work, I ran a hyperparameter sweep and GPU learning experiments, auditing result coverage and retaining failures for numerical-stability analysis.

02

My contributions

  1. 01

    PeTeR and reproducible experiment materials

    I co-authored PeTeR, accepted at the UAI 2026 Workshop on Tractable Probabilistic Modeling. I also prepared a supplementary experiment entry point with a short guide, benchmark data, and CSV table outputs to make the experiment workflow easier to inspect.

  2. 02

    A sweep that retains its failures

    I executed and audited 1,120 configurations at each of K=1, 3, and 5, totaling 3,360. Some high-learning-rate settings produced invalid probabilities. I retained their error records and confirmed the handling with Adrian Ciotinga; the expected numerical failures were delivered alongside the other results.

  3. 03

    Match GPU resources to the workload

    In a separate learning experiment across 28 datasets, warmup runs and memory telemetry informed concurrency. K=3 ran eight concurrent jobs on L40S; the higher-memory K=5 workload used A100 80GB. I checked scheduler state, error output, result files, and repository changes before each batch delivery.

  4. 04

    Inspect results beyond job completion

    While monitoring experiments, I noticed disordered dataset images and anomalous data and reported them to Adrian. Alongside file-coverage checks, I used visualization and preserved diagnostic logs so that a completed job was still open to examination.
