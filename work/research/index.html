Question & approach

The question

Can a trained probabilistic circuit become more robust to distribution shift without its original training data or retraining from scratch?

The approach

PeTeR addresses this question through post-training robustification. As a co-author, I contributed experiment execution, result validation, engineering support, and supplementary experiment materials. In subsequent work, I ran a hyperparameter sweep and GPU learning experiments, auditing result coverage and retaining failures for numerical-stability analysis.

02

My contributions

  1. 01

    Check probability constraints

    I identified probability-normalization drift under direct optimizer updates and validated post-update clamping and renormalization while preserving gradient propagation.

  2. 02

    Rule out an evaluation confound

    I investigated label alignment in random-embedding evaluation, repeated the analysis with fixed labels, and ruled out label mismatch as the cause of poor clustering.

  3. 03

    PeTeR and reproducible experiment materials

    I co-authored PeTeR, published at the UAI 2026 Workshop on Tractable Probabilistic Modeling. The extended manuscript is under review at AISTATS 2027. I connected training and evaluation across codebases through compatible model serialization and prepared a reviewer-facing runner with packaged datasets and CSV outputs.

  4. 04

    A sweep that retains its failures

    I executed and audited a PeTeR sweep covering 3,360 hyperparameter configurations. Some high-learning-rate settings produced invalid probabilities. I retained their error records and confirmed the handling with the collaborator; the expected numerical failures were delivered alongside the other results.

  5. 05

    Match GPU resources to the workload

    In separate GPU experiments across 28 datasets, I reconstructed the CUDA/PyTorch/Triton/PyJuice runtime. Warmup runs and memory telemetry informed concurrency: eight concurrent jobs on L40S for one experiment set, and A100 80GB for the higher-memory set. I checked scheduler state, error output, result files, and repository changes before each batch delivery.

  6. 06

    Inspect results beyond job completion

    While monitoring experiments, I noticed disordered dataset images and anomalous data and reported them to Adrian. Alongside file-coverage checks, I used visualization and preserved diagnostic logs so that a completed job was still open to examination.
